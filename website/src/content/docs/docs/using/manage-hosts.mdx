The navigation rail, the column of icons down the left edge of the sidebar, shows your ordinary session hosts. It shows
your Mac, captioned **Local**, and each remote Linux machine or Mac you added, each with its mark, a short name, and a
dot that says whether Farhelm can reach that host right now. An amber key on a host's mark means it is waiting for you
to sign in. Point at a host's mark to see its full name. To keep every
name in view, select **»** (Expand navigation rail) at the very bottom of the rail: the rail widens beside the session
list, and **«** **Collapse** narrows it again. Farhelm remembers which you chose. Choosing a host
there shows only its sessions, and **All** shows every host's again ([Read the session list](/docs/using/session-list/)).
The **Hosts** button at the foot of the rail swaps the session list for the host list, where your Mac is listed as
**Local (this machine)** and everything on this page happens; choose a host or **All** on the rail to go back. Adding a
host is covered in [Add a remote host](/docs/get-started/add-a-remote-host/).

The bell just above **Hosts** shows [notices about a host](/docs/using/session-list/#notices-about-a-host), starting with
trashed checkouts whose files could not be freed. It turns red with an unread count. Opening and closing it quiets the
bell while keeping the problem listed until it is resolved. Host setup and update problems are not listed there; they
show on the host's mark and in the rows above your sessions, described next.

Hosts with a problem, an update, setup still running, or a sandbox or container being deleted also appear above your
sessions, so you can keep track of them
while you work. While a setup, update or uninstall runs, its row names the current step; hover anywhere on the row to
see every step and the reason for any problem. Select a row or its **Details** button to open the host list at that
host. The list disappears when nothing needs attention and while the full host list is open.

A host waiting for you to [sign in](#sign-in-to-a-host) shows **Waiting for sign-in** with **Answer**, or **Sign-in
needed** with **Sign in** once a question expired, was cancelled or failed. A row that needs you, such as a sign-in, a failed setup or update, or
a host Farhelm cannot confirm, keeps an amber edge, and when it first appears a short amber flash draws your eye to it
without taking your keyboard focus.

## The host menu

<Screenshot
  name="manage-hosts/host-menu"
  alt="A remote host's menu, opened from its three dots, with settings, uninstall, and remove explained."
/>

Open it from a host's `⋯` in the host list, or right-click the host's mark on the navigation rail. **Update** installs
your Mac's version of Farhelm on the host; [Update a host](#update-a-host) has more. Other items appear only when a host needs attention, such as setting
Farhelm up on a host that does not have it yet, or **Take over** on a host another Farhelm manages
([Take over a host from another Farhelm](#take-over-a-host-from-another-farhelm)). Your Mac's own row has no **Uninstall** or **Remove**. In the Farhelm
app installed on your Mac, its **Update** checks for a new release of Farhelm and installs it, as
[Update or uninstall Farhelm](/docs/using/update-and-uninstall/) describes. In the Linux app, or in a copy of Farhelm
that is not installed in `~/Applications`, it is greyed out instead. If **Retry** or **Adopt** fails, a dialog shows
why.

A [Tensorlake sandbox](/docs/using/tensorlake-sandboxes/) has **Delete sandbox** in place of **Remove**, and no
**Uninstall**: Farhelm owns that machine, so deleting it loses its files and sessions.
[Suspend or delete a Tensorlake sandbox](#suspend-or-delete-a-tensorlake-sandbox) covers it. A
[container session](/docs/agents/containerized-sessions/#stopping-and-deleting)'s host has **Delete container**,
which removes its whole environment and files. The menu of a sandbox or a container also has **Suspend**, or **Resume**
once it is suspended, and a suspended one shows **Resume** on its row too;
[Suspend and resume for a sandbox](/docs/using/tensorlake-sandboxes/#suspend-and-resume) explains what each keeps and
when Suspend asks first, and [Container sessions](/docs/agents/containerized-sessions/#stopping-and-deleting) covers a
container's. A container session's container, like
a [sandbox session](/docs/using/tensorlake-sandboxes/#give-each-session-its-own-sandbox)'s sandbox, appears in Hosts and
update notices without a permanent rail icon.

## Host settings

<Screenshot
  name="manage-hosts/host-settings"
  alt="A host's settings dialog, with the destination and alias edit buttons, the icon choice, and its five permission switches explained."
/>

Choose **Settings** in a host's menu to open its settings. The destination and alias save with their field's **Save**,
and the checkout folder with its own; everything else saves as you change it.

The destination is what you would type after `ssh`, such as `dev@build-box` or a name from your ssh configuration. The
alias is only for display: it changes how the host is shown everywhere in Farhelm, up to 64 characters, and nothing on
the host itself. Without an alias, the navigation rail and the session list show a short name instead: the first part
of the hostname, or the whole IP address. The local Mac's destination and a Tensorlake sandbox's destination cannot be changed. **Start YOLO sessions without asking** is the same choice the
YOLO confirmation offers when you [start a session](/docs/using/start-a-session/#confirm-a-yolo-launch).

For an SSH host, **Connection → Tailscale host** changes how Farhelm reaches the host and verifies its SSH key.
Changing it reconnects the host. [Add a remote host](/docs/get-started/add-a-remote-host/) explains what the host needs.

**Manage sessions on this host without asking** lets agents in this host's sessions create, spawn, copy, rename, stop,
restart and delete sessions on this same host. A copy's source must be here too. It starts off on every host, including
Tensorlake sandboxes. Requests for other hosts, template changes, host changes and
links are outside this permission. Farhelm must still be open, because the helm, the program that shows all your hosts'
sessions and serves the UI, runs inside the app and handles these requests
([The pieces](/docs/how-it-works/the-pieces/)).

**Run farhelm commands without asking** also covers session actions on every other host and changes to your launch
templates. Changing one switch does not change the other; you can change either while the broader permission is on.
[Approve what agents do](/docs/using/approve-agent-requests/#stop-asking-for-a-host) explains their limits: wrapper
prefixes can still need your own approval, host changes and
[new sandboxes or containers](/docs/using/approve-agent-requests/#new-sandboxes-and-containers) always do, and YOLO or custom-command launches still need the
separate YOLO permission. A plain restart, including of a YOLO session, needs no YOLO permission.

**Open links without asking** and **Open local-network links without asking** are separate permissions for opening this
host's sessions' links in your browser. Both start off, and the second includes your machine and private network. [Open
links from a remote session](/docs/using/approve-agent-requests/#open-links-from-a-remote-session) explains the cards
and why allowing local-network links needs a separate choice. Adopting a reinstalled or
replaced host turns all five permissions off again.

For any host, choose an icon and a color under **Appearance** to tell its sessions apart at a glance. The mark
appears on the navigation rail, beside its sessions, in the host list and quick switcher, and in the session launcher's
host picker. Each choice saves
as you make it. Your Mac starts with a red laptop mark, which you can change.

Under **Checkouts**, **Checkout folder** chooses where this host's new managed checkouts go. Leave it empty to use the
default shown in grey from **Settings → Checkouts**. Save an empty field, or choose **Use default**, to follow that
default again. This field needs **Save**; it can be changed while the host is disconnected or suspended, and existing
checkouts stay where they are. [Start from a managed checkout](/docs/using/start-a-session/#start-from-a-managed-checkout)
covers launching one.

In Settings, **Color terminal frames by host** under **Sessions** adds a thin frame around every terminal in its host's
color. It starts off. The terminal background and text colors stay the same. The switch is kept by the
[helm](/docs/how-it-works/the-pieces/) and takes effect at once.

## Sign in to a host

A host can ask you to sign in when Farhelm connects to it: for a verification code, a password, a key passphrase, or to
confirm a host key it has not seen before. This happens when Farhelm opens, when it reconnects after a dropped
connection, when you add the host, and before **Update**, **Uninstall** or **Re-run** start. Farhelm keeps two
connections to each host, one for sessions and one for setup and updates, so a new host can ask twice; the question says
which one it is for, **For sessions** or **For setup and updates**.

Questions wait in the row above your sessions, with an amber key on the host's mark on the navigation rail, so they never
take your keyboard while you are typing elsewhere. Choose **Answer** to open the question beside its row. A question
from the first connection after you open Farhelm can open by itself, ready for your answer, as long as you have not
started typing or clicking yet. Your answer is hidden until you choose **Show**. **Send** or Enter answers, and
**Cancel** stops that connection attempt. When the host wants a security key touched and SSH says so, Farhelm shows that
instead of a field. Your answers go straight to SSH; Farhelm never stores or logs them.

A question you do not answer within two minutes expires. After that, or after you cancel or signing in fails, the row
reads **Sign-in needed** and Farhelm waits for you to choose **Sign in** to try again. Unavailable sessions on that host
say so on their card, with **Sign in** there too. To hear a chime when a host asks, keep **SSH sign-in** turned on under
**Settings → Notifications**.

## Take over a host from another Farhelm

Each host remembers which Farhelm manages it. A host nobody manages yet goes to the first Farhelm that connects to it.
If you add a host that another Farhelm manages, such as one on another computer, the **Add host** dialog asks whether to
**Take over host** and names the other Farhelm and when it last connected. A host already in your list reads
**Managed by another Farhelm** instead, and its menu offers **Take over**.

Taking over makes this Farhelm the host's manager. The host's sessions keep running, and the other Farhelm loses access
to them until it takes the host back. Until you take a host over, Farhelm refuses to set it up, update it or uninstall
Farhelm from it. This is also how you get your hosts back if you lost your list of hosts, for example after deleting
Farhelm's data: add each host again and confirm **Take over**. Farhelm always asks before taking over, even with **Set
up new hosts without asking** turned on, and an agent's [request to add a host](/docs/using/approve-agent-requests/#host-changes)
cannot take one over.

## Update a host

When a host has an update, its mark on the navigation rail and the **Hosts** button at the rail's foot show an
up-arrow. Above your sessions, each update row offers **Update**. If several listed hosts have updates, **Update all**
updates those hosts. While automatic updates are off, **Always update** there also updates the listed hosts and turns on
**Update remote hosts automatically**, so later updates install without asking. If that choice cannot be saved, a
message says later updates will still wait, with **Try again**. You can dismiss optional update reminders; required updates stay visible because that host's
sessions cannot be reached until it is updated. The reminders return when another host gains an update, or when you quit
and open Farhelm again.

When a remote host runs an older version of Farhelm than your Mac, an **↑ Update** button appears beside its name: amber
when updating is optional, red when the host cannot connect until it is updated. Hover over it to see both versions.
Clicking it updates the host straight away, without asking, and shows the progress in its place. **Update all** at the
top of the full host list updates every remote host whose Update action is available.

Updating a host does not interrupt its sessions: they keep running through the update. This includes remote Macs;
**Update**, **Update all** and automatic updates use the same flow for them. A remote Mac with the Farhelm desktop app
installed cannot be updated this way, even though you can use Farhelm already running there; update Farhelm from the desktop app on that Mac instead.
If Farhelm cannot plan an update, a dialog shows the reason before anything changes. If the host asks you to
[sign in](#sign-in-to-a-host) first, its row above your sessions offers **Answer**. While the update runs, its progress
appears above your sessions; hover over that row to see each step. A failed update appears there with **Details**, and the host's mark on the rail shows a
warning until you open the host list, where its row shows what happened.

Farhelm updates remote hosts automatically while the app is open. This starts on for new installations, and when you
upgrade from a release that left it off it turns on again, even if you had turned it off. To keep updates manual, turn off **Update remote hosts
automatically** in Settings, under **Hosts**. The setting applies to every remote host. Your Mac has its own update
setting. If an automatic update of a host fails, Farhelm does not try that host again
until it reconnects or Farhelm restarts. The host keeps its up-arrow, so you can update it yourself.

## Uninstall Farhelm from a remote host

This applies to SSH hosts. A Tensorlake host has [Delete sandbox](#suspend-or-delete-a-tensorlake-sandbox) instead.

**Uninstall** first shows what it will remove from the host: the [supervisor](/docs/how-it-works/the-pieces/)'s service
and Farhelm's program files, each with its path there. On a remote Mac this removes its background job and the job's
file in `~/Library/LaunchAgents`. It also names the directory that holds the host's Farhelm data, which it keeps, and
stops the small Farhelm processes that keep its terminals running. Nothing changes until you select **Uninstall** in
that dialog. Once it is done, the host is gone from the list. A short line above it confirms the
removal; hover over the line to see where the data remains, or use its Copy button to copy that directory's path. You
can dismiss the line with ×. Delete the directory on the host yourself if you want the data gone too.

Uninstall never stops a session for you. If any session on the host has not ended (it is running, waiting or idle, or
its status is not known yet), or still has a terminal tab open, it says which ones and does nothing. Stop those sessions
first; Stop also closes their terminal tabs, as in [Stop, restart, and resume](/docs/using/stop-restart-resume/). Do not start a
session on the host while the uninstall runs: one started then can end along with Farhelm. Uninstall also needs Farhelm
to be connected to the host so it can check. If its row shows **Unreachable, retrying**, **Needs update**, **Too new**,
**Identity mismatch**, **Identity unverified**, **Duplicate** or **Managed by another Farhelm**, fix that first: the refusal says how, and so do the
host's [details](#see-more-about-each-host). **Remove** still works. A few less common setups are
refused too, with the reason shown in a dialog: a remote Mac with the Farhelm desktop app installed, one whose Farhelm
runs from somewhere unexpected (such as a Linux host set up with an earlier release's `farhelm helm setup`), and
one whose
`~/.local/lib/farhelm` directory is a symbolic link or has something mounted on or inside it. On a Mac the mount check is
best effort: a mount whose name contains " (" or " on " can go unnoticed. **Remove** still works for all of them.

If something goes wrong partway, the host stays in the list and its details show which steps finished. Choose
**Uninstall** again to finish the job; it picks up where it stopped. Once a failed uninstall has removed the service
and Farhelm's supervisor there has stopped, the next one can finish even though the host no longer answers.

To remove Farhelm from your Mac, see [Uninstall Farhelm](/docs/get-started/uninstall/).

## Remove a host

<Screenshot
  name="manage-hosts/remove-dialog"
  alt="The remove host dialog, with the remove, don't ask again button explained."
/>

**Remove** in an SSH host's menu asks first.

For an SSH host, removing it only makes Farhelm forget it. Nothing is stopped or uninstalled on the host, and
adding the same destination again brings back its sessions, though their pins, and which ones you had already looked
at, start fresh. **Cancel** or Escape leaves the host where it was.
Removing a container host, changing its destination, or turning its **Tailscale host** on or off requires cleaning up
the environments and recipe images recorded there first. A refusal names what needs cleanup.
[Container sessions](/docs/agents/containerized-sessions/#stopping-and-deleting) explains their confirmed deletion.

The dialog stays open until Farhelm answers; if removal is refused, it shows why. If you chose to skip the confirmation,
a refusal shows under the host's row in the host list instead.

## Suspend or delete a Tensorlake sandbox

Choose **Suspend** in a sandbox host's menu to suspend it, which stops its CPU and memory billing while keeping its
files and processes. Tensorlake can also suspend a sandbox host itself, for example on the Usage Credits plan after 24
hours without a connection from Farhelm; [Tensorlake sandboxes](/docs/using/tensorlake-sandboxes/#suspend-and-resume)
explains more. A suspended host shows a grey **Suspended** state with **Resume** on its row, and an open session on it
offers **Resume host**. While it resumes, it shows **Resuming…** and then **Connecting**.

**Delete sandbox** permanently deletes the sandbox, its files, sessions and snapshots taken from it. It always asks,
regardless of **Remove hosts without asking**. Keep work you need elsewhere first. While Farhelm deletes it, the host
shows **Deleting…**. If deletion fails, the host stays
listed with the reason; try **Delete sandbox** again to finish. This needs a saved Tensorlake API key. Its recipe and
the recipe's prepared snapshot remain available for new hosts.

## Turn the questions back on

<Screenshot
  name="manage-hosts/app-settings"
  alt="The Settings dialog with Hosts selected, showing the section list and the switches for asking before setting up or removing hosts."
/>

The gear sits at the foot of the navigation rail, below **Hosts**. The switches cover only actions you start in the UI;
[agents' host requests](/docs/using/approve-agent-requests/#host-changes) still need your approval. These two apply to
SSH hosts, and a change takes effect at once. Tensorlake creation has its own
**Create Tensorlake sandboxes without asking** switch under **Tensorlake**; deleting a sandbox always asks.

[Settings](/docs/using/settings/) describes its other sections.

## See more about each host

<Screenshot
  name="manage-hosts/details"
  alt="The host list with Details switched on: each host's Farhelm version and sessions, and the destination behind an alias."
/>

A host's row also opens by itself while a setup
runs on it, or when its last setup or update failed.

A Tensorlake host's details also show its sandbox name, recipe, CPU and memory, and tunnel mappings and status. A failed
tunnel makes its dot amber and shows the reason. A suspended host's details say when suspension was noticed and whether
it happened outside Farhelm. [Tensorlake sandboxes](/docs/using/tensorlake-sandboxes/) covers its recipes and account
settings. A [container environment](/docs/agents/containerized-sessions/) has its own entry in Hosts and update
notices, without a permanent navigation rail icon. Its details identify the recipe, container host, tunnel status and
credential delivery warnings.
